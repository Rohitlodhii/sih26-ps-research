# SIH26058 — Low-Power Real-Time Adaptive Software-Defined Sonar Transmitter Payload for AUVs (MoES)

- **Organization:** Ministry of Earth Sciences
- **Category:** Hardware | **Theme:** Robotics and Drones
- **Difficulty Score:** 8.5/10

## What the PS is about
AUV side-scan sonars use fixed pulses; real needs vary — high-frequency chirps (500 kHz) give resolution but fail in muddy/deep water, low-frequency (100 kHz) penetrates but is blurry. The transmitter should adapt its analog waveform in real time like an SDR, without draining the AUV battery.

## What to build
A physical hardware prototype (not simulation):
- Embedded firmware (STM32/ESP32/TI DSP/FPGA; C/C++/Verilog) using DMA + hardware timers to stream wave arrays to a DAC without CPU stalls
- On-the-fly modulation: LFM chirps, geometric sweeps, phase-coded pulses
- Environmental sensor interface (real sensors or potentiometer-simulated inputs) instantly tuning bandwidth/center frequency, pulse duration, amplitude
- Analog frontend: active/passive low-pass filters + op-amp amplification, plus digital windowing (Hamming/Hann/Blackman) to smooth pulse edges and kill sidelobes
- Validated on oscilloscope/spectrum analyzer with clean FFT spectrograms; low-power, marine-drone-ready form factor

## Key challenges
Mixed DSP+analog design, real-time DMA-based waveform synthesis, filter/window design, power optimization.

## Why this difficulty
Deep embedded + RF/analog hardware with strict real-time and power constraints — excellent but demanding electronics work.
